This release sends large files through a direct TLS connection between the client and the agent, outside the Remote Desktop connection, when the server opens its ports for it.

## Why

Every transfer I measured in production went through the UltimateRDP channel at 2 to 4 MB/s, on links that carry three times more. The dense encoding of 0.5 already removed the padding; what is left is the flow control of the Remote Desktop connection over its virtual channels, and nothing in UltimateRDP can change it. A direct connection avoids it.

## What happens now

- When the agent decides a fast transfer, it listens on the first free TCP port of a range, 3400 to 3449 by default, for this transfer only. Its answer to the drag gives the port, a single use token and the SHA-256 of its certificate, through the channel.
- After the drop the client connects to the server on that port, accepts only that certificate, sends the token, then the files. The progress window and the status bar show "Direct connection".
- When the connection cannot be made within 3 seconds, as through a Remote Desktop Gateway or a closed firewall, or fails before the first data is acknowledged, the files go through the channel as in 0.5.2, without anything to do.
- Both logs say which path a transfer took, the port, the TLS protocol and the rate.

## For administrators

Open inbound TCP 3400 to 3449 on the Windows firewall of the server and on the firewall of your hosting provider, only from the client addresses allowed for RDP. The README gives the exact rule. Without it, everything keeps working through the channel. The tray menu entry "Direct connection for large files" and the settings `directTransfer` and `directPortRange` turn it off or move the ports.

## Upgrade

Install 0.6.0 on both sides for the direct connection. A client or an agent 0.5.2 keeps using the channel with the other side at 0.6.0. I tested the direct connection over loopback so far, at about 320 MB/s for 500 MB; please send me both logs of a transfer on your network.
